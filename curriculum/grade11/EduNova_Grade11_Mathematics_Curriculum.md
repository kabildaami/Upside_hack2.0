## Learning sequence

## G11-M01 — Functions, domains and variation

**Learning goal:** Explain concepts rigorously, calculate accurately, and justify each conclusion.

### Definition — function

A function f from D to R assigns each x in D exactly one real number f(x). Its domain D contains the admissible inputs.

### Definition — increasing

f is increasing on I when for all a,b in I with a < b, f(a) <= f(b); strictly increasing means f(a) < f(b).

### Theorem — monotonicity using derivatives

If f is differentiable on the interior of I and f' >= 0 throughout I, then f is non-decreasing on I. If f' > 0 throughout I, f is strictly increasing.

### Guided worked problem

For f(x)=x^2-4x+3, find its domain, compute f'(x)=2x-4, and determine the variation intervals. Justify the sign of the derivative before concluding.

### Misconception to discuss

Common error: a positive function is not necessarily an increasing function. Use the sign of the derivative, not the sign of f.

### Written assessment template

1. Identify assumptions and unknowns. 2. State a theorem with its hypotheses. 3. Write each transformation with a justification. 4. Verify and conclude.

## G11-M02 — Quadratic equations and sign charts

**Learning goal:** Explain concepts rigorously, calculate accurately, and justify each conclusion.

### Definition — discriminant

For ax^2+bx+c=0 with a nonzero, the discriminant is Delta=b^2-4ac.

### Theorem — real roots

If Delta>0 there are two distinct roots (-b±sqrt(Delta))/(2a); if Delta=0 one double root -b/(2a); if Delta<0 there are no real roots.

### Theorem — sign of a trinomial

When two real roots r1<r2 exist, ax^2+bx+c has the sign of a outside [r1,r2] and the opposite sign inside (r1,r2).

### Guided worked problem

Solve x^2-5x+6 <= 0 by computing Delta=1, finding roots 2 and 3, and recording the sign of the trinomial.

### Misconception to discuss

Common error: do not reverse the sign chart without checking the leading coefficient.

### Written assessment template

1. Identify assumptions and unknowns. 2. State a theorem with its hypotheses. 3. Write each transformation with a justification. 4. Verify and conclude.

## G11-M03 — Sequences and mathematical induction

**Learning goal:** Explain concepts rigorously, calculate accurately, and justify each conclusion.

### Definition — arithmetic sequence

A sequence is arithmetic when u(n+1)=u(n)+r for a fixed r. Thus u(n)=u(0)+nr.

### Definition — geometric sequence

A sequence is geometric when u(n+1)=q u(n), with u(n)=u(0)q^n.

### Principle — mathematical induction

To prove P(n) for every integer n>=n0, prove the base case P(n0) and prove that P(n) implies P(n+1).

### Guided worked problem

For u(0)=3 and u(n+1)=u(n)+4, conjecture u(n)=3+4n and prove it with a base case and induction step.

### Misconception to discuss

Common error: an inductive step without a verified base case is incomplete.

### Written assessment template

1. Identify assumptions and unknowns. 2. State a theorem with its hypotheses. 3. Write each transformation with a justification. 4. Verify and conclude.

## G11-M04 — Limits and continuity

**Learning goal:** Explain concepts rigorously, calculate accurately, and justify each conclusion.

### Definition — limit at a point

lim(x->a) f(x)=L means values of f(x) approach L whenever x approaches a within the domain, independently of f(a) if that value exists.

### Definition — continuity

A function f is continuous at a if f(a) is defined and lim(x->a)f(x)=f(a).

### Theorem — intermediate value

If f is continuous on [a,b] and k lies between f(a) and f(b), there exists c in [a,b] with f(c)=k.

### Guided worked problem

For f(x)=x^2-2, justify that a root lies between 1 and 2 using continuity and the intermediate value theorem.

### Misconception to discuss

Common error: sign change alone is insufficient unless continuity on the interval is established.

### Written assessment template

1. Identify assumptions and unknowns. 2. State a theorem with its hypotheses. 3. Write each transformation with a justification. 4. Verify and conclude.

## G11-M05 — Derivatives, tangents and optimization

**Learning goal:** Explain concepts rigorously, calculate accurately, and justify each conclusion.

### Definition — derivative

f'(a)=lim(h->0)(f(a+h)-f(a))/h, provided the limit exists.

### Theorem — tangent equation

If f is differentiable at a, the tangent at (a,f(a)) has equation y=f(a)+f'(a)(x-a).

### Criterion — local extremum

A change of the derivative sign from positive to negative at a point gives a local maximum; negative to positive gives a local minimum.

### Guided worked problem

For f(x)=x^2-6x+8, find the derivative, solve f'(x)=0 and determine the minimum; write the tangent equation at x=2.

### Misconception to discuss

Common error: setting f'=0 gives a candidate, not an automatic minimum.

### Written assessment template

1. Identify assumptions and unknowns. 2. State a theorem with its hypotheses. 3. Write each transformation with a justification. 4. Verify and conclude.

## G11-M06 — Trigonometry on the unit circle

**Learning goal:** Explain concepts rigorously, calculate accurately, and justify each conclusion.

### Definition — sine and cosine

On the unit circle, the point at angle t has coordinates (cos t,sin t).

### Identity — Pythagorean identity

For every real angle t, sin^2(t)+cos^2(t)=1.

### Theorem — periodicity

sin(t+2pi)=sin(t), cos(t+2pi)=cos(t).

### Guided worked problem

If sin(t)=3/5 and t is in the first quadrant, use sin^2(t)+cos^2(t)=1 and the quadrant to determine cos(t).

### Misconception to discuss

Common error: the square-root sign requires quadrant information.

## G11-M07 — Vectors and analytic geometry

**Learning goal:** Explain concepts rigorously, calculate accurately, and justify each conclusion.

### Definition — vector coordinates

For A(xA,yA), B(xB,yB), vector AB=(xB-xA,yB-yA).

### Definition — scalar product

For u=(ux,uy), v=(vx,vy), u dot v=ux vx+uy vy.

### Theorem — perpendicularity

Two nonzero vectors in the plane are perpendicular exactly when their scalar product is zero.

### Guided worked problem

Given A(1,2), B(4,6), C(5,1), compute AB and AC, and test whether the vectors are perpendicular using their dot product.

### Misconception to discuss

Common error: a zero coordinate does not imply perpendicularity.

## G11-M08 — Probability and conditional reasoning

**Learning goal:** Explain concepts rigorously, calculate accurately, and justify each conclusion.

### Definition — probability

On a finite equiprobable sample space, P(A)=number of favorable outcomes / number of possible outcomes.

### Definition — conditional probability

When P(B)>0, P(A|B)=P(A and B)/P(B).

### Theorem — total probability

If B and its complement partition the space, P(A)=P(A|B)P(B)+P(A|not B)P(not B).

### Guided worked problem

A box has 3 blue and 2 red tokens. Two tokens are drawn without replacement. Calculate the probability that both are blue by multiplying the conditional probabilities.

### Misconception to discuss

Common error: without replacement the second draw probabilities change.
